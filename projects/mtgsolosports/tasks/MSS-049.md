# MSS-049 — Expand records with more detailed scoring achievements

## Goal

Make the records/statistics experience more interesting by exposing meaningful **single-round, single-stage, league-specific, individual Cup, and team-event scoring records**, instead of limiting records to only a small set of high-level achievements.

The records should highlight exceptional performances at the level where they actually happened and make it easy to understand **who or which team holds the record, what the value is, and in which competition/season/stage/round it happened**.

## Context

- Project: `mtgsolosports`; target repository: `AlexBDevCorner/MtgSoloSports`; default branch: `main`.
- MtgSoloSports already has historical competition data and a records/statistics experience, but many interesting performances are not currently surfaced as records.
- A single outstanding round, dominant stage, unusually high league-point total, or exceptional team group stage should remain discoverable after the season has finished.
- Records should come from authoritative persisted competition/history data rather than from only the currently active season.
- Follow `AGENTS.md`, `docs/game-rules.md`, the existing vertical-slice architecture, and established records/statistics UI conventions.

## Requirements

1. Expand the records view with additional scoring/performance record categories.

2. At minimum, add records for:
   - **Most athlete points in a single league round**.
   - **Most athlete points in a single league stage**.
   - **Most league points**, tracked in the appropriate league context rather than mixing unlike competitions together.
   - **Most athlete points in a single Colour Cup individual round**.
   - **Team scoring records**, including meaningful single-round/event totals where the data model supports them.
   - **Most team points in a single team group stage**.

3. Treat the categories above as the required baseline, not an exhaustive list. Audit the existing competition types and historical score data for other clearly analogous records that are useful and understandable, for example:
   - strongest individual round performances in other individual Cups;
   - strongest team round performances;
   - strongest full group-stage team totals;
   - strongest stage totals where a competition has a stage concept;
   - other score-based records that can be calculated reliably from persisted results.

4. Keep records scoped correctly. Do not compare values across competition formats where the scoring systems or number of opportunities are materially different. Where appropriate, maintain separate records by:
   - league;
   - Cup/event type;
   - individual vs team competition;
   - round/stage/group-stage scope.

5. Each displayed record must provide enough context to make the achievement understandable. Where applicable show:
   - athlete or team;
   - record value;
   - record category;
   - season;
   - competition/event;
   - league;
   - stage;
   - round;
   - group/team context.

6. Preserve ties. If multiple athletes or teams share the same record value, do not arbitrarily hide all but one unless the existing records system has a clearly documented tie convention.

7. Records must be calculated from historical persisted results so that:
   - old seasons contribute;
   - records remain visible after a new season starts;
   - later league/team membership changes do not rewrite historical record ownership.

8. Reuse the existing records/statistics infrastructure and visual language where practical rather than building an unrelated parallel feature.

9. Keep the records page readable despite the larger number of categories. Group related records sensibly, for example by League, individual Cups, and team Cups/events.

10. Do not change any competition scoring rules, simulation logic, league-point rules, team selection, qualification rules, or event formats as part of this task.

## Architectural direction

- Start from the existing records/statistics slice and extend its data model/query rather than introducing a second independent records subsystem.
- Prefer explicit record definitions/categories over scattered ad-hoc calculations in UI components.
- Derive records from persisted round/stage/event results whenever those already contain the authoritative score.
- Where a record needs aggregation, keep the aggregation in the backend/application slice and expose a presentation-ready DTO to the frontend.
- Make category scope explicit so future records can be added without accidentally comparing incompatible competitions.
- Avoid storing duplicate "record" rows if the record can be deterministically derived from authoritative historical result data, unless the existing architecture deliberately persists record snapshots.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The records/statistics UI includes a record for most athlete points in a single league round.
- [ ] The records/statistics UI includes a record for most athlete points in a single league stage.
- [ ] League-point records are available in the correct league-specific context and do not mix incompatible leagues/competitions.
- [ ] The records/statistics UI includes most points in a Colour Cup individual round.
- [ ] Team-event scoring records are represented.
- [ ] The records/statistics UI includes most team points in a single team group stage.
- [ ] Other obvious analogous score-based records supported by existing historical data are included where they improve the records experience.
- [ ] Every record shows its holder/value and enough season/competition context to identify where it happened.
- [ ] Tied record holders are handled deterministically and are not silently lost.
- [ ] Historical seasons contribute to records without replaying competitions.
- [ ] Records remain historically correct after athletes change league/team or become inactive.
- [ ] Record categories do not compare scores across materially different scoring formats.
- [ ] Existing records and competition behavior continue to work.
- [ ] Backend/frontend tests cover representative individual, league, team, historical, and tie cases.

## Verification

1. Run the standard backend build/test checks documented in `AGENTS.md`.
2. Run the frontend checks documented by the repository, including typecheck/tests/build where applicable.
3. Verify the record holder for a known high-scoring league round.
4. Verify the record holder for a known high-scoring league stage.
5. Verify league-point records are separated/scoped correctly.
6. Verify a Colour Cup individual-round record.
7. Verify team round/group-stage records from completed team competitions.
8. Verify an older season can still hold a record after advancing to later seasons.
9. Verify two equal best values are handled correctly.
10. Verify existing records and normal competition simulation/scoring are unchanged.

## Out of scope

- Changing how round, stage, league, Cup, or team points are calculated.
- Rebalancing competition formats or scoring scales.
- Changing qualification, promotion/relegation, or team formation rules.
- Building a generic analytics/dashboard system unrelated to sporting records.
