# MSS-038 — Make Live reveal the primary above-the-fold view with side controls

## Goal

On the **Live event** page, put the actual card reveal front and center from the moment the page loads. On an ordinary desktop display, users should see the reveal board and be able to start manually turning over cards **without scrolling past multiple stacked management panels**. Move competition management and round navigation into a compact side area, and use the existing generous desktop width for the spectator experience.

## Context

- Project: `mtgsolosports`; target repository: `AlexBDevCorner/MtgSoloSports`, branch `main`.
- User's 1536 × 806 desktop screenshot (2026-09-28) shows three full-width vertical sections: **Live competition** (league, Next Round, Complete Stage, explanation, progress), **Rounds** (round selectors), and **Animated reveal** (title, modes, explanation, playback controls, progress). Even though the reveal section starts in the screenshot, **no actual MTG card from the reveal board is visible without scrolling**.
- `MSS-035` introduced the wide-screen 32-card visual reveal board; keep its image-first, appropriately large cards and responsive grid. `MSS-037` made Live reveal manual by default; retain that behavior. Both are completed prerequisites.
- IMPORTANT clarification from user: the 1536 × 806 screenshot is NOT evidence of the entire available monitor width. The real browser/monitor has **substantial additional empty space on both the left and right outside the visible screenshot**. The implementer MUST inspect and remove any unnecessarily restrictive overall application/page max-width or centered-wrapper constraint on the Live experience, not merely redistribute content inside the currently narrow column. Use the actual available wide-screen browser viewport, preserving modest comfortable edge gutters; verify this explicitly at wide desktop sizes.
- Current Live page implementation: `src/MtgSoloSports.Web/src/features/live/LivePage.tsx` renders two management `Card` sections, then the shared `RoundReveal`. `src/MtgSoloSports.Web/src/features/reveal/RoundReveal.tsx` renders the reveal header, explanatory text, playback toolbar, progress, latest-card spotlight, `RevealBoard`, and collapsible detail table. Review `src/MtgSoloSports.Web/src/styles.css` for layout and reveal styles.
- The shared `RoundReveal` is also used by History replay. Do not accidentally force Live-specific sidebar/layout or manual-play behavior onto History.
- Follow `AGENTS.md`, `docs/technical-design.md` and current frontend feature-local conventions. This is a React/CSS UX task; **no MediatR**, no sporting-rule or backend changes.

## Requirements

1. Replace the Live page's vertical stack of large full-width panels with a desktop layout that has **a narrow side panel for management** and a **large, top-aligned main reveal area**. Place league selection, Next Round, Complete Stage, completed-round count and selectable persisted rounds in the side panel (one compact panel or a pair of compact stacked panels). Keep the selected league, current round and available actions obvious.
2. The main content region must begin with the currently selected **actual reveal board**, not just the title of a tall Animated reveal panel. Keep the round title, Instant/Animated choice, playback controls (Play/Pause, Restart, −1, +1, Show all, speed) and progress easily discoverable in a **compact toolbar/header** above or beside the board. Users should not have to scroll past large gaps or instructional paragraphs to get to the cards.
3. When a card is revealed, the existing latest-card spotlight must **not push the card board down or cause the main board to jump out of view**. Integrate the spotlight in available side space, a compact stable-height spot or an unobtrusive overlay/adjacent area; do not obscure cards or controls. Reduce always-visible explanatory copy/checksum clutter by using a short hint, subdued metadata or an accessible Help/details disclosure. Retain essential action errors and gate/disabled-state explanations near their associated actions.
4. Treat the 1536 × 806 reference screenshot as a **specific usability regression test**: with an existing save and a completed round selected, at normal browser zoom and at initial page scroll position, at least the **first full visible row of real card tiles** (face-down initially or revealed during play) must appear inside the viewport, along with the primary manual `+1` action. Seeing only the Animated reveal header/toolbar does not count. Do **not** shrink cards to tiny thumbnails or require the entire 32-card board to fit vertically; normal scrolling for later rows and detailed results is acceptable.
5. **Use the full available desktop browser width, not the width implied by the cropped reference screenshot.** Audit the app shell's container/max-width and Live page wrapper (including parent containers), eliminating artificial centered-column constraints that leave huge unused margins on both sides. Keep only sensible page gutters; validate against actual browser viewport measurements at 1920 and 2560px. Preserve the wide-board outcome from `MSS-035`. On sufficiently wide screens (including practical 1920 and 2560px layouts), the main area should remain dominant and the 32-athlete completed board should still be capable of eight reasonably large, legible cards per row × four rows. At narrower desktop widths, use responsive column counts or adjust side panel sizing rather than squashing card imagery or creating horizontal overflow. Only add a second side rail for spotlight/playback if it does not sacrifice card readability; a single management sidebar plus compact main toolbar is preferable to three cramped columns.
6. On narrower tablets/phones, collapse or reflow the side layout into a compact, touch-friendly page; keep reveal and manual stepping prominent and competition/round actions accessible (e.g. compact expandable controls). No overlapping sticky controls, clipped menus, nested horizontal scrolling or hidden focus targets. Any sticky behavior must respect viewport height and the app header.
7. Preserve all Live flows: changing league/selected persisted round, Next Round and Complete Stage busy/disabled/error states, default selection of the latest completed round, no-round/empty states, reload, and athlete profile navigation. A newly completed Live round must still open as **manual, paused, 0/N** (except explicit Instant and reduced-motion paths); only deliberate Play starts autoplay. History must retain its existing replay semantics, reveal controls and shared presentation logic.
8. This is **presentation only**: maintain the existing immutable persisted reveal data, correctness of revealed/pending states, score/movement presentation, progress and reduced-motion behavior. No simulation calls due to UI changes, no change to RNG or save mutations. Prefer page-specific Live layout composition and small opt-in shared reveal presentation changes over duplicating `RoundReveal` or rebuilding the app shell.

## Architectural direction

- Start with `features/live/LivePage.tsx`, `features/reveal/RoundReveal.tsx` and relevant styles; a focused Live-layout wrapper/sidebar and carefully scoped adjustments to the shared reveal component are expected. Do not duplicate playback state, `RevealBoard` or backend hooks.
- Let the large board own the flex/grid main column (`min-width: 0` as needed); keep side management at a sensible fixed/clamped width. Use responsive CSS Grid/container breakpoints rather than hard-coded assumptions about every browser size.
- Avoid large blank margins or tall cards created only to preserve the old visual sections. Put primary live actions and playback within comfortable reach, while making the board the hero.
- Keep semantic landmarks/headings, accessible names, visible focus, keyboard operation, adequate tap targets and truthful live progress announcements. No animation-dependent layout shifts.
