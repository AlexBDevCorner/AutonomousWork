# MSS-051 — Add a visual feeder-league rebalance reveal

## Goal

Make the end-of-season **feeder-league rebalance** feel like a visible sporting event instead of an invisible roster update.

Add a dedicated reveal that shows **how each feeder league changes after Superleague movement is resolved**, which athletes are affected, why they moved, and how every feeder league returns to its required roster size.

The experience should make it easy to follow athletes leaving for Superleague, returning from Superleague, being sent to the common pool, and being drawn from the common pool into an underfilled league.

## Context

- Project: `mtgsolosports`; target repository: `AlexBDevCorner/MtgSoloSports`; default branch: `main`.
- Feeder rebalancing happens after all Superleague movement and qualifier outcomes are resolved.
- According to `docs/game-rules.md`, each feeder league is rebalanced in this order:
  1. remove athletes entering Superleague;
  2. add returning athletes of that sporting colour;
  3. if the league is above 32 athletes, send the lowest-ranked remaining athletes from the previous feeder season to the common pool;
  4. if the league is below 32 athletes, randomly select eligible athletes from that colour's common pool until the league contains 32.
- Pool selection is equal-probability and former league athletes may return immediately.
- Because multiple different movement reasons can occur in the same rebalance, the UI should explain the sequence rather than showing only a final roster.
- MtgSoloSports already has reveal-oriented event UI. Reuse its interaction language, athlete imagery, and card/profile-link conventions where practical.
- Follow `AGENTS.md`, `docs/game-rules.md`, the existing vertical-slice architecture, and established frontend styling conventions.

## Visual direction

Use a **rebalance board** that tells the roster-change story league by league.

For each affected feeder league, show a compact **before → movements → after** presentation rather than rendering all 32 athletes as a dense roster.

A suggested reveal sequence is:

1. **Starting roster**
   - Show the league name and starting roster count.
   - Keep the current league visually anchored throughout the sequence.

2. **To Superleague**
   - Reveal athletes leaving the feeder league because they qualified for/entered Superleague.
   - Their athlete tiles move upward/out of the feeder league and receive an explicit **TO SUPERLEAGUE** label.

3. **Returning from Superleague**
   - Reveal athletes returning to this feeder league from Superleague.
   - Their tiles move down into the league with a **RETURNING** label.

4. **Roster check**
   - Visibly update the league count after those movements, for example **30 / 32** or **34 / 32**.
   - This makes the reason for the next step immediately understandable.

5. **Common-pool adjustment**
   - If the league is above 32, reveal the affected lowest-ranked athletes one by one as they leave the league and move into the **COMMON POOL**, labelled **TO COMMON POOL**.
   - If the league is below 32, show the open roster slots first, then reveal the actual randomly selected common-pool athletes one by one and move them into the league, labelled **DRAWN FROM POOL**.
   - Treat pool draws as a small reveal moment: the selected athlete identity can be hidden briefly and then revealed before the card moves into the league. Do not imply that the UI itself is performing the random draw; the authoritative result must already be known.

6. **Balanced**
   - Finish the league with a clear **32 / 32 — BALANCED** state.
   - Leave a compact summary of every affected athlete and their movement reason.

When several feeder leagues are affected, progress through them one league at a time or in another clearly grouped sequence. The user should always know which league is currently being rebalanced and how far through the overall rebalance event they are.

The animation should communicate roster movement. Avoid decorative animation that does not help explain where an athlete came from or went to.

## Requirements

1. Add a dedicated visual presentation for the feeder-league rebalance event.

2. Show the rebalance in the same logical order as the sporting rules:
   - departures to Superleague;
   - returns from Superleague;
   - overflow to the common pool;
   - common-pool draws for vacancies.

3. For every affected athlete, clearly show:
   - athlete identity;
   - source;
   - destination;
   - movement reason/type.

4. Distinguish at least these movement types:
   - feeder league → Superleague;
   - Superleague → feeder league;
   - feeder league → common pool;
   - common pool → feeder league.

5. Make the league roster count part of the presentation.
   - The user should be able to see how the league becomes overfilled or underfilled after Superleague movement.
   - The final state must clearly show that the feeder league is back at 32 athletes.

6. Focus the animated/reveal portion on **changed athletes** rather than forcing the user to inspect all unchanged members of a 32-athlete roster.

7. For an underfilled league, reveal the actual common-pool selections individually enough that the draw feels meaningful.
   - Use the authoritative draw result from the completed rebalance.
   - Do not reroll or reproduce random selection logic in the frontend.
   - Revisiting the event must reveal the same athletes.

8. For an overfilled league, make it clear that the athletes moved to the common pool are the athletes selected by the existing lowest-ranked-remaining rule.
   - Presentation may show their prior league position/rank where available and useful.
   - Do not recompute the sporting decision in the frontend.

9. Show a visible **Common Pool** destination/source during pool-related movements. This can be a shared side/bottom area, drawer, or other compact visual device; it does not need to render the complete pool roster.

10. If a feeder league requires no pool adjustment after Superleague movements, show that outcome cleanly rather than fabricating an extra step.

11. If a feeder league has no changes at all, either skip it in the progressive reveal with a clear **No changes** summary or show a very compact unchanged state. Do not make users click through long empty sequences.

12. After all leagues are processed, show a persistent event summary grouped by feeder league with:
   - athletes who left for Superleague;
   - athletes who returned from Superleague;
   - athletes sent to the common pool;
   - athletes drawn from the common pool;
   - final roster count.

13. The event summary should make the overall amount of churn understandable, for example the total number of affected athletes and per-league movement counts.

14. Provide deliberate reveal controls consistent with the rest of MtgSoloSports.
   - Users should be able to advance without waiting through long automatic timers.
   - Revisited/completed historical events must be inspectable directly in their completed state.

15. Use existing athlete/card imagery and profile links where available so affected athletes are visually recognizable and can be explored after the reveal.

16. Do not rely on colour alone to distinguish movement types. Use labels, direction, icons/arrows, spatial movement, or equivalent cues.

17. Respect reduced-motion preferences. The same sequence and information must remain understandable with restrained transitions or immediate state changes.

18. Keep the rebalance view responsive. The movement story, athlete identity, counts, and controls must remain usable on both wide desktop screens and narrow layouts.

19. Historical rebalance events must display the **actual persisted historical movements**, especially common-pool draws. They must not be reconstructed from current athlete league membership or by running random selection again.

20. Preserve all existing feeder-rebalance rules, ranking rules, random pool-selection rules, eligibility rules, Superleague outcomes, and final league assignments. This task is primarily presentation plus any result persistence/API metadata needed to reproduce the historical event exactly.

## Architectural direction

- Start from the existing season-transition/rebalance slice and determine where the authoritative rebalance decisions are currently made.
- The frontend must consume explicit movement results rather than infer them from before/after current rosters.
- Prefer a presentation-ready rebalance result model containing entries such as:
  - athlete;
  - source location/league;
  - destination location/league;
  - movement type/reason;
  - feeder league;
  - sequence/order where needed;
  - relevant previous-season rank for overflow decisions if already available.
- If the application currently persists only the final league memberships and does **not** retain enough information to reconstruct the exact historical rebalance, extend persistence so the completed event records its actual movements and pool draws.
- Random common-pool selections must be performed once by the existing domain/backend process and persisted/returned as results. UI replay must be deterministic.
- Reuse existing reveal state/controls/components where practical rather than creating a parallel reveal framework.
- Consider a reusable athlete-movement tile/model shared with other season-transition visuals where doing so remains simple, but do not couple unrelated sporting rules.
- Keep all sporting calculations and movement decisions in the current backend/domain logic.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Feeder rebalancing is presented as a dedicated visual reveal rather than only an invisible roster update or static final list.
- [ ] The reveal follows the sporting sequence: Superleague departures, Superleague returns, overflow handling, then pool draws.
- [ ] Every affected athlete has an explicit source, destination, and movement reason.
- [ ] Athletes entering Superleague are visibly distinguishable from athletes returning from Superleague.
- [ ] Athletes sent to the common pool are clearly shown leaving the feeder league for the pool.
- [ ] Athletes drawn from the common pool are revealed and clearly shown entering the feeder league.
- [ ] The league roster count visibly changes through the sequence and ends at 32 / 32.
- [ ] The UI focuses the reveal on changed athletes instead of rendering an unreadable full-roster animation.
- [ ] A league needing no pool adjustment is handled correctly.
- [ ] A league with no changes does not create a long empty reveal step.
- [ ] Multiple affected feeder leagues are presented in a clear sequence/grouping.
- [ ] The completed event has a persistent per-league summary of all movements.
- [ ] The completed event exposes an understandable overall churn summary/count.
- [ ] Revisiting a historical event shows the exact original common-pool draws and movements without rerolling or deriving them from current membership.
- [ ] Reduced-motion users receive the same information without depending on animation.
- [ ] The layout works at existing wide desktop and narrow responsive widths.
- [ ] Existing rebalance outcomes and all sporting rules remain unchanged.
- [ ] Backend/frontend tests cover underfilled, overfilled, exactly-balanced, unchanged, multiple-league, and historical replay cases.

## Verification

1. Run the standard backend build/test checks documented in `AGENTS.md`.
2. Run the frontend checks documented by the repository, including typecheck/tests/build where applicable.
3. Complete a season where at least one feeder league loses athletes to Superleague and verify the departure reveal and count change.
4. Verify a returning Superleague athlete enters the correct feeder league.
5. Verify an overfilled feeder league reveals the correct athlete(s) moving to the common pool and finishes at 32.
6. Verify an underfilled feeder league reveals the exact common-pool athlete(s) chosen by the backend and finishes at 32.
7. Verify a feeder league that reaches exactly 32 after Superleague movements skips unnecessary pool adjustment cleanly.
8. Verify an unchanged feeder league is skipped or summarized without unnecessary reveal steps.
9. Verify multiple feeder leagues can be processed without mixing athletes between league contexts.
10. Complete the reveal and confirm the final per-league summary matches the authoritative persisted results.
11. Reopen the completed event and verify the same historical movements and pool draws are shown.
12. Verify the view at a wide desktop width and a narrow viewport.
13. Verify reduced-motion mode preserves the full information and reveal progression.

## Out of scope

- Changing the order or rules of feeder rebalancing.
- Changing the 32-athlete feeder-league size.
- Changing Superleague qualification, promotion/relegation, qualifier results, or league-colour rules.
- Changing how lowest-ranked overflow athletes are selected.
- Changing common-pool eligibility or equal-probability random selection.
- Redesigning unrelated league tables, athlete profiles, or Cup events.
- Adding a general-purpose animation framework solely for this feature.
